# Ballad slice (SB), 19 Oct 2026

Two sincere piano ballads examined as whole works: Lewis Capaldi's "Someone You Loved" (SB-SONG1) and Adele's "Someone Like You" (SB-SONG2). The pairing has a documented link: TMS used the piano sound of "Someone Like You" as the tonal target for "Someone You Loved" (SB-S8). The two songs also turn out to rest on opposite documented decisions about scope, which became the main distinction.

## What I verified

- **"Someone You Loved".** Released 2018-11-08 (SB-S1). Writers are Tom Barnes, Lewis Capaldi, Pete Kelleher, Benjamin Kohn and Sam Roman, from GRAMMY.com's Song of the Year nomination (SB-S2). Phil Plested is not credited. Wikipedia's infobox lists an unexplained extra name, so I did not rely on it.
- **"Someone Like You".** Released 2011-01-24 (SB-S24). Writers are Adele Adkins and Dan Wilson, per the 21 liner notes as cited by Wikipedia.
- **First-person writer accounts.**
  - Capaldi: SB-S3, SB-S5, SB-S12.
  - TMS: Sound On Sound Inside Track (SB-S8, both pages) and Billboard (SB-S7).
  - Sam Roman: SB-S4. It is brief and only says the song was written at the end of a session.
  - Dan Wilson: SB-S25 to SB-S28, SB-S30 and SB-S31.
  - Adele's words appear only through relays (Wikipedia, Songfacts).
- **Conflicts recorded, not resolved.** The Capaldi-side accounts disagree on who developed the chorus melody (Capaldi alone in his shed, or Barnes at the piano). They also disagree on how long the session took (half an hour, two hours or three hours) and on whose idea it was to widen the subject. No source attributes any line to any one writer. Treatment matches every other writer: no special status.

## Three most useful distinctions

1. **Open frame vs personal frame, and how each is built.**
   - Capaldi widened "Someone You Loved" from one breakup to loss in general, so it fits a breakup and a bereavement. The mechanism is grammar, not vagueness: the title is second person and past tense, with the narrator as the object of the other person's love (SB-F1, SB-F5).
   - Wilson and Adele made "Someone Like You" as personal as possible, with a married ex and an uninvited visit (SB-F6).
   - Both travelled. Proposed card 1.
2. **One qualifier carries the irony.** In Wilson's reading, "like" turns the brave resolve into a confession, so the chorus can be sung straight (SB-F7). Proposed card 2.
3. **Restraint is one decision at two scales.** Plain lyric, plain bed, and each addition tested against the voice:
   - "Someone You Loved": TMS rejected drums and cut the strings back to the middle eight (SB-F3).
   - "Someone Like You": the demo was kept over an orchestral version (SB-F8).
   - "Stay With Me": Sam Smith refused a bridge the label asked for because the song had already said what it needed. This is the first documented writer's reason for the "cut it" side of card C45 (SB-F12).

Also worth flagging: Fender deliberately did not write his grief song as a slow ballad (SB-F14). It is a limit on treating the ballad as the default for loss.
