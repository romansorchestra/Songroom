## The most specific findings

1. **One word in the title.** "Good Luck, Jane!" became "Good Luck, Babe!" because "Babe" felt more sarcastic and playful. Roan had wanted a proper name, and the writers argued about it (PA-F6).
2. **Same song, the verses.** The early verses were sharper and more barbed, and were deliberately rewritten to feel effortless and casual. Roan asked for a carefree image of arms out of a sunroof. The chorus was fixed by putting chosen words in full voice and changing the key, not by rewriting the words. The bridge was improvised alone in about two minutes over looped pre-chorus chords (PA-F7, PA-F8, PA-F9).
3. **Espresso.** The hook began as a hummed melody. The room doubted that its silly line would survive. A verse excuse line came straight from the late-working studio. The title arrived in a 20-minute sung burst, followed by hours of word edits (PA-F1, PA-F2).
4. **"Too far" as a signal.** Amy Allen says the moment that raises a "too weird" flag is usually the best part of the song. Carpenter says much of Short n' Sweet came from jokes they couldn't stop laughing at. The limit is in PA-F15: critics found the pun and innuendo density on Man's Best Friend dulling, and said "Tears" reached for a memeable lyric (PA-F5, PA-F15).
5. **Inverting a trope.** Finneas found it fun to reverse the love-till-death trope in "Birds of a Feather", and Eilish slipped "something dark" into a sweet song. She also nearly cut it as "kind of stupid" (PA-F11, PA-F12).
