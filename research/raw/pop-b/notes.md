## Most specific findings
1. **Laughter as the filter (PB-F1, Song Exploder transcript).** Abrams and Hobert often keep the line that makes them laugh hardest, and laughing is how they get through hurt feelings. On the same transcript, Abrams labels one verse as the petty part, admits it hurt its subject, and keeps the bridge for her own admission, with a deliberate change of rhythm (PB-F2).
2. **No rhyme for inner monologue (PB-F10, Song Exploder 2026).** Charli XCX left rhyme out of an embarrassing interior-monologue lyric because "rhyme requires a bit of premeditation" and would make the thought sound revisited rather than spontaneous. Note this is the 2026 song "Camera", not brat.
3. **A reply verse turns a monologue into a dialogue (PB-F9).** Lorde says Charli's song "made me say things that I had never said". A critic hears the reply working because it accepts blame instead of matching the spiral.
4. **Plain words, lifted by structure (PB-F14, PB-F15, PB-F16).** Some solemn-sounding lines in Ordinary started as jokes about a dusty house. Producer Adam Yaron gives the lift to structure: chorus written first, mismatched verses, key raised seven semitones, a low chorus start then an octave jump. Critics call the same plainness generic, while listeners use it for weddings and memorials.
5. **The petty, checkable detail (PB-F6).** Hobert's rule is to say plainly what she wants to say. Her favourite Sue Me line rests on an ex's clothing brand. Paste counters that the approach can read as an affected diary-pop pose with a brute-force chorus.
