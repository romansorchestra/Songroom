# songs-songwriter: Stick Season (Noah Kahan) and People Watching (Sam Fender)

## Verified
- **Stick Season**: released 2022-07-08, written by Noah Kahan alone (ST-S4, existing). Produced by Kahan and Gabe Simon.
- **People Watching**: single released 2024-11-15 on Polydor, written by Sam Fender alone (SS-S3 infobox; SS-S4 track listing). Title track of *People Watching*, released 2025-02-21. Producers: Fender and Adam Granduciel, with Joe Atkinson and Dean Thompson as additional producers. UK peak No. 4, debuting on the chart of 22 November 2024; BPI Platinum.
- **Seventeen Going Under** (profile only): single released 2021-07-07, written by Fender alone, produced by Bramwell Bronte. UK peak No. 3 on the chart of 7 January 2022. Won the 2022 Ivor Novello for Best Song Musically and Lyrically (SS-S7).

## Three most useful distinctions
1. **Displacement chorus** (People Watching, documented rewrite):
   - Before: the chorus that stalled for months explained what the town and tragedy had done to the narrator.
   - After: the chorus that worked watches strangers, and borrows a grave inscription ("Somebody's Sweetheart") to name one of them as loved.
   - Effect: someone hearing only the chorus would not know anyone had died. That breaks card C43's test on purpose.
2. **Set the music to where the lyric ends, not to its subject:**
   - Fender chose a euphoric feel for grief so the music would reach acceptance.
   - He says he prefers yearning, hopeful writing to happy writing.
   - Kahan wanted Stick Season heard as hopeful because the season passes.
3. **Local facts in the chorus, each paired with an admission anyone could make** (Stick Season; analyst inference, low confidence):
   - Kahan's chorus holds the place, the ex's mother and the Christmas homecoming. His verse holds the portable image (the missed exit).
   - This is a documented counterexample to C43.

Also usable, with weaker evidence:
- People Watching holds back "you" (the dead friend) until the late sections.
- In the Stick Season sketch, the clinical self-label ("narcissistic") became a confessed behaviour ("play the victim").
