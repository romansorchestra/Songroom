# ADÉLA / PRIMA: research notes (prefix AD)

Accessed 2026-10-10. No audio or video was used: everything comes from text pages.

## What was verified
- **Release:** PRIMA, ADÉLA, Capitol Records (Polydor in the UK and on some vinyl), released **2026-09-04**. Apple Music US shows 4 Sept, while the Lebanon storefront and one Qobuz edition show 3 Sept, probably a time-zone display difference. It has 11 tracks and runs about 31 minutes, and (P) 2026 UMG Recordings.
- **Tracklist** (from the label press release, AD-S3, matching AllMusic and AOTY): 1 KGB, 2 Nicole Kidman, 3 I'm The Man, 4 Boys, 5 Red Bottoms, 6 Hitachi, 7 Fantasize, 8 Starving Artist, 9 Marijuana, 10 Therapy, 11 Ain't In LA.
- **Single dates** (Qobuz): KGB 2026-04-16, Red Bottoms 2026-06-11, Ain't In LA 2026-07-22.
- **Birth date:** 27 Nov 2003, Bratislava (Clash and Wikipedia), not "c. 2004" as the brief said.
- **Credits:**
  - Apple Music showed **no** credits in the fetched text. Curl to Apple Music, Qobuz, Wikipedia, LOBF and Slant was refused by the proxy (403).
  - The only full per-track writer/producer table is Wikipedia's (AD-S4), and **it has no citation**.
  - Credits corroborated by a second source:
    - "Ain't In LA": Jergová, Walsh, Theron Thomas, Slatkin, Brady (Songfacts).
    - "Hitachi": Jergová, Michaels, Walsh, Brady, Slatkin; produced by Slatkin (Just Jared).
    - "Nicole Kidman": Jergová, Michaels, Walsh, Uffie/Anna-Catherine Hartley, produced by Slatkin and Brady (Songfacts). Wikipedia also lists Slatkin and Brady as writers. That Uffie is Hartley comes from my own knowledge, not a fetched page.

  - The "Therapy" producer column on Wikipedia looks wrong: it lists Walsh and Michaels as producers.
- **Charts:**
  - US Billboard 200: debuted at No. 4 with 56K units. This is from a secondary source; the chart week isn't stated.

## Discrepancies flagged
- Wikipedia says ADÉLA called her lyrics "exaggerated / over-the-top" and cites The FADER (2025-08-25). I re-checked that FADER page and **the wording isn't there**, so I didn't use the claim.
- The Golden Globes / "Nicole Kidman" quote is credited to W Magazine by W and the label, but to Rolling Stone by Songfacts and HollywoodLife. Capital FM says she watched Eyes Wide Shut before the party; Songfacts says after.
- On Capital's "Making The Album" page the song headings come *after* the quote blocks, so which song each anecdote belongs to is ambiguous. That affects the Hitachi session story and the Lara Raj quote.
- Metascore was 73 from 7 reviews per Wikipedia, but 68 from 9 on the live Metacritic page. AOTY gives 69 from 10.

## Most specific findings
1. **"Therapy" was cut down from a ramble** (NPR via KAXE). The first take was about 15 minutes of her talking over the beat, recorded with her voice nearly gone, then edited to 3 minutes. Critics then called it the weakest song: "especially simple", "a slog", and the lowest user track score on AOTY. (AD-F6)
2. **The Hitachi session** (Capital). An upbeat session went wrong; Blake mentioned a Hitachi; they wrote a deliberately bad upbeat song, then turned it into a sad song about intimacy fading. The off-topic joke became the title and the song went the opposite way. (AD-F3)
3. **"Red Bottoms" came from a worn-out shoe** (Vogue CS). The luxury-brand title hides a feeling of being run down. (AD-F1)
4. **"Nicole Kidman" names a feeling, not a subject.** She sums the song up as feeling like Nicole. (AD-F4)
5. **Critics split on "Ain't In LA"** (Singles Jukebox, 17 critics, average 5.76). Some say the only named place is LA, so it's too vague; others call it a truly great chorus. (AD-F10)

## What she says, what reviewers say, and my inference
- **ADÉLA's own statements** (9 findings), from Vogue CS, NPR/KAXE, Capital FM, W, Clash and the label release:
  - Bluntness is a Slovak trait in her view, and dry humour is how she copes.
  - She writes in solitude first, then finishes ideas in co-writes.
  - Many bad songs come before a good one.
  - "KGB" was meant literally and as a joke.
  - LA is a metaphor in "Ain't In LA".
  - Lorde's "Royals" and "Team" were her model for "Ain't In LA".
  - She used to avoid love songs.
- **Reviewers' descriptions** (5 findings):
  - Spoken banter with Lara Raj on "Boys".
  - A Theron Thomas pitched-down refrain on "Ain't In LA".
  - Verse 1 is about one girl and verse 2 widens to many girls.
  - Critics disagree on whether she works best winking or deadpan.
  - The ballads and sincere songs drew the most criticism.
- **My inference** (1 finding): her proper-noun titles each stand for an emotional state rather than the song's topic. No source says this was a deliberate method.
