# songs-pop (prefix SP): two whole-song examinations

Accessed 2026-10-19. Web search and the fetch tool only. No audio, video or browser tools. Every fetched page is tool-extracted text.

## Songs
- **SP-SONG1 "Please Please Please"**, Sabrina Carpenter. Released 2024-06-06. Written by Carpenter, Amy Allen and Jack Antonoff (AA-S18, which cites the Short n' Sweet liner notes). Function: a comic-desperate plea to one man.
- **SP-SONG2 "Ain't In LA"**, ADÉLA. Released 2026-07-23 (Apple Music and the label release; Qobuz shows 07-22, probably a time-zone difference). Written by Jergová, Billy Walsh, Theron Thomas, Blake Slatkin and Dylan Brady; produced by Slatkin and Brady (Qobuz track credits, SP-S3). Function: a stance and identity anthem.
- **Why "Ain't In LA" and not "Nicole Kidman":**
  - It has a full printed lyric with section labels (Capital FM, AD-S18).
  - It has writer commentary from five outlets.
  - Its critics' discussion is more detailed.
  - Commentary on "Nicole Kidman" stops at one Golden Globes anecdote (SP-S11).

## Three most useful distinctions
1. **Register descent within one chorus** (SP-F2, SP-F3; proposed card 1). "Please Please Please" goes from the politest to the bluntest in a fixed order:
   - the polite title
   - a petty makeup stake
   - "heartbreak is one thing", which ranks heartbreak below ego
   - the profane word last

   The writers say the last word was an ad lib, sung with the melody pushed lower. The title stays sweet and repeatable while the edge lands at the end.
2. **Pronoun ladder for turning a tribute into an anthem** (SP-F8). "Ain't In LA" moves:
   - from a third-person portrait (inspired by her mother) in verse 1
   - to "you" in the repeated pre-chorus
   - and holds the narrator's "I" until the chorus.
3. **A negated place-title leaves a blank** (SP-F10; proposed card 2). The song names only the place it rejects. Listeners filled the blank with their own hometowns, while critics called it vague. This qualifies C41's advice to "name at least one place": naming or leaving the place blank is a choice, and each option has a cost.

Also notable:
- In "Please Please Please", the fantasy verse is set in a different key, and the return to the chorus pops the bubble (SP-F1; proposed card 3).
- The narrator is built to be pitied and feared at once (SP-F6).

## How the two songs differ
| | "Please Please Please" | "Ain't In LA" |
|---|---|---|
| Addressee | One man, in a scene | A crowd of girls |
| Narrator | First person throughout | Third, then second, then first person |
| Engine | Jokes and petty stakes | Earnest; the attitude comes from the title's negation and a second voice |
| Specifics | Personal and comic (makeup, his acting) | Confined to verse 1; the rest stays general |
| Structure | Late-added bridge (sincere line, then joke); key change for the fantasy | No bridge; a male refrain is the only contrast |
| Origin | A phrase she was already carrying, plus chorus chords | A family photo, a Lorde model, and frustration with LA |

## Gaps
- **No documented before/after wording for either song.** "Please Please Please" has only two documented changes: the chorus ending was moved lower, and the bridge was added a month later. For "Ain't In LA", a critic's guess that an earlier draft named Bratislava is not evidence.
- **Who wrote which part.** Unknown, apart from verse 2 of "Please Please Please" (Carpenter and Allen). It is also unknown whether Thomas wrote the refrain he performs.
- **Sources seen only through tool extraction.** Song Exploder and both printed lyrics were read only this way, and the extractions were inconsistent in places, as noted above.
- **No audio.** Every musical statement is a writer's or a press description.
