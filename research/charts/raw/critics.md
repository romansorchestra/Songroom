# Critics and industry writers on the "diaristic / conversational female pop" cohort, 2023 to 2026

Raw research notes for Sam's private file. Compiled 11 Oct 2026.
Question: is pop "mostly female relationship angst, very conversational, echoing the Instagram-poet style", and what else is big?

## How to read these notes (method and honest caveats)

- Every point is my own paraphrase. Quotes are critic sentences under 25 words, attributed. No song lyrics are quoted or paraphrased anywhere; songs are described by style and subject only.
- Source tags: **[R]** = page fetched and read directly. **[2]** = reported secondhand (a Wikipedia critical-reception table, the list.co.uk "what the reviewers say" roundup, Newsweek roundup, or Pajiba quoting reviews). I could not open Pitchfork, NYT, Guardian, New Yorker, Atlantic or Vulture review pages directly: the tool only allows URLs that surfaced in search, and those outlets' own pages did not surface. So their views are mostly **[2]**. Re-check anything quoted before publishing it.
- Page text was condensed by a small summariser model, so quote fragments could be slightly off from the original wording. Treat quotes as "as reported".
- Not found despite searching: any Switched On Pop, Penny Fractions, Bandsplain or Pop Pantheon piece on this cohort; any major-outlet critic drawing a direct Instapoetry/Rupi Kaur comparison to Gracie Abrams, Olivia Rodrigo, Sabrina Carpenter, etc. (see section 1H). The "Instagram poet" half of the hypothesis is **not something critics are saying** in anything I could reach.
- Dates: pre-2023 items are marked "pre-range" and used only as context.
- Several search results were SEO spam ("lyrics explained" farms on odd domains). None used.

## 1. LYRIC STYLE

### 1A. Gracie Abrams: diaristic, conversational, specific (and the pushback)

- Rolling Stone's review of *The Secret of Us* says her writing is more evocative and grown-up than the debut, and reads many songs as open letters to an ex, or like a late-night text to a close friend. Frames it as "sad-girl pop" with brighter melodies. [R, paraphrase of reviewer] — *Gracie Abrams Takes Her Sad Girl Pop to the Next Level on The Secret of Us*, Rolling Stone. https://ca.rollingstone.com/music/gracie-abrams-takes-her-sad-girl-pop-to-the-next-level-on-the-secret-of-us/
- Stereogum calls her frank, conversational lyrics her "secret weapon" on "Risk" and notes the co-write with Audrey Hobert; delivery is trembling over glassy production. [R] — *Gracie Abrams, "Risk"*, Stereogum. https://stereogum.com/2261804/gracie-abrams-risk/music
- Slant (Dana Poland) praises her gift for putting emotion into words and the glimpses of her inner life, but finds the sound monotonous across the record. Compares the instrumentation to Swift. [R; also summarised in Wikipedia] — *Gracie Abrams, The Secret of Us*, Slant. https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/gracie-abrams-the-secret-of-us-album-review
- PopMatters-style review (outlet unconfirmed in the fetched text) groups Abrams with Swift and Rodrigo as a diaristic writer whose confessions come with built-in sympathy; says pop-song structure is the container for a direct style, and that bridges "advance the argument" (e.g. escalating through insults and self-questioning on "us."). [R, outlet attribution uncertain] — *Gracie Abrams, The Secret of Us*. https://popmatters.com/gracie-abrams-secret-of-us
- Clash: catchy, Swift-like hooks, Bridgers influence, but the lyrics are sometimes conventional and not at the literary level of some peers. [R] — *Gracie Abrams, The Secret of Us*, Clash. https://www.clashmusic.com/?p=397285
- Atwood Magazine roundtable: critics hear Gracie start from real stories (hers or friends') and push them to extremes; praise escalating outros and the best bridge on "I Love You, I'm Sorry"; no one uses "diaristic" or "conversational" literally. Notes she wrote with live singalongs in mind. [R] — *Roundtable: The Secret of Us*, Atwood. https://atwoodmagazine.com/tsou-gracie-abrams-the-secret-of-us-album-review/
- The sharpest critical counter I found: an Atwood essay faults *The Secret of Us* for lacking specificity and texture, contrasting Phoebe Bridgers's observation; on vulnerability: "Vulnerability glows when it's brave enough to be unlikable." Also argues songwriting is "a negotiation of characters", i.e. confession is always constructed. [R] — *Notes on the Confessional: Diaries, Dissonance, and Becoming in Modern Indie & Pop*, Atwood Magazine. https://atwoodmagazine.com/essay-notes-on-the-confessional-diaries-dissonance-and-becoming-in-modern-indie-and-pop/
- *Good Riddance* (2023) reception, via Wikipedia: Line of Best Fit called the writing "both hyperspecific and vague" (characters without context); Pitchfork's Jane Bua found some lyricism clichéd; Rolling Stone's Maya Georgi noted harsh lines delivered in a gentle whisper; Harvard Crimson called it stereotypical. [2] — *Good Riddance (album)*, Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Riddance_(album)
- Third album *Daughter From Hell* (July 2026), mixed to negative from the big UK/US outlets as reported by Pajiba: Guardian (Laura Snapes) calls it bloodless, pretty music against dark lyrics, "struggle to pick her out of a lineup" in spirit; Pitchfork calls the vagueness "frustrating". [2, via a polemical blog] — *The Vulnerable Blandness of Gracie Abrams*, Pajiba, 31 Jul 2026. https://www.pajiba.com/celebrities_are_better_than_you/the-vulnerable-blandness-of-gracie-abrams.php
- Same album, positive: Rolling Stone (via CA site) hears the chatty style of "That's So True" carry into "Minibar" and calls the writing more vivid and assured; Spin (Lina Lecaro) hears "diary-like energy", a conversational rhythm in the vocal and less blame than before. [R] — *Gracie Abrams album review: Daughter From Hell*, Rolling Stone CA. https://ca.rollingstone.com/music/music-album-reviews/gracie-abrams-album-review-daughter-from-hell/ ; *Gracie Abrams Is Wistfully Reflective on Daughter From Hell*, Spin. https://www.spinmagazine.com/2026/07/gracie-abrams-is-wistfully-reflective-on-daughter-from-hell/
- Same album, middling: NME says the dark imagery often feels forced and works best when turned humorous; best songs are Dessner co-writes. Dazed: strong diaristic gift and vivid metaphors, but stuck in teenage perspective and repetitive heartbreak imagery, unfavourably compared with Rodrigo's growth. Hot Press: the album "mistakes big emotions for emotional depth". [R] — NME https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/gracie-abrams-daughter-from-hell-review-3957468 ; Dazed https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/70665/1/five-best-tracks-gracie-abrams-daughter-from-hell-new-album-review ; Hot Press https://www.hotpress.com/music/album-review-gracie-abrams-daughter-from-hell-23153398
- Alt Press frames her honesty as confessional songwriting plus a diary-like online persona (resurfaced old diary entries), small tangible domestic details, and self-accountability (she doesn't cast herself as the victim). [R] — *How Gracie Abrams became the brutally honest face of Gen Z pop*, Alt Press. https://www.altpress.com/?p=194884

### 1B. Audrey Hobert (Gracie's co-writer): the conversational engine

- NPR segment describes Hobert's songs as funny, conversational, hyperspecific, diary-like, written in a stream-of-consciousness voice-memo manner; she told Gracie to write whatever came to mind first, and the piece credits that with the wit of Gracie's biggest hits. [R] — *Pop music's funniest newcomer is Audrey Hobert*, NPR via WAMC, 13 Aug 2025. https://www.wamc.org/2025-08-13/pop-musics-funniest-newcomer-is-audrey-hobert
- Clash (8/10) calls her debut comedic, very specific, diaristic, and "relatable", while noting some repetition of themes. [R] — *Audrey Hobert, Who's The Clown?*, Clash. https://www.clashmusic.com/?p=432688

### 1C. Olivia Rodrigo: blunt, funny, talk-sung

- Grammy.com: GUTS keeps the teenage-diary feel of *Sour* but is sharper; conversational "someone talking through a problem" verses with fast mood shifts; humour and sarcasm; most credits are only Rodrigo and Dan Nigro. [R] — *Olivia Rodrigo GUTS: new album takeaways*, Grammy.com. https://www.grammy.com/news/olivia-rodrigo-guts-new-album-takeaways-lessons-review/
- NME (5/5): "compellingly unflattering" candour about a failed romance, blunt self-analysis, jabs at an ex that get sharper and funnier. [R] — *Olivia Rodrigo, Guts review*, NME. https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/olivia-rodrigo-guts-album-review-vampire-3493907
- Roundup of GUTS reviews: Pitchfork 8.0 ("a familiar cast of antagonists": bad boys, social anxiety, self-image); Guardian 4/5; NME 5/5 on turning vulnerability into a call to action; Spin praised the "unfiltered candour of a teen girl's diary entries". [2] — *What the reviewers say: Olivia Rodrigo, Guts*, list.co.uk. https://list.co.uk/news/what-the-reviewers-say-olivia-rodrigo-guts-44082
- Wikipedia's GUTS reception table: Clash (Alex Barry) called the lyrics complex and verbose, delivered in fast talk-singing; NYT's Jon Caramanica "poignantly fraught"; Guardian's Alexis Petridis "sharp and witty"; Rolling Stone's Rob Sheffield "instant classic"; Daily Telegraph (Poppie Platt) saw little growth; LA Times's Mikael Wood praised comic delivery that shifts between naturalistic, screwball and deadpan. [2] — *Guts (album)*, Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guts_(album)
- Third album *you seem pretty sad for a girl so in love* (12 Jun 2026): AP-syndicated review calls it a concept album following a romance from first date to dissolution and "a giant step forward for the songwriter". A Stanford Daily piece on the pre-release singles (31 May 2026, https://stanforddaily.com/2026/05/31/celebrating-girlhood-with-olivia-rodrigo/) notes the turn from angry breakup songs toward love songs. Teen Vogue (as summarised) says the sound outruns the writing. Atwood roundtable: more mature, specific and restrained, "no one is painted as the villain", bridges called exceptional. [R / 2] — AP review https://www.goshennews.com/news/national_news/music-review-olivia-rodrigos-you-seem-pretty-sad-for-a-girl-so-in-love-is/article_a54e074a-c0e7-59b9-8902-a595154c9088.html ; Teen Vogue via https://pulse.byoviral.com/trend/2026-06-17/or3-is-a-great-album-if-you-don-t-look-too-hard-at-the-lyrics ; Atwood https://atwoodmagazine.com/ysps-olivia-rodrigo-you-seem-pretty-sad-for-a-girl-so-in-love-album-review-roundtable/

### 1D. Sabrina Carpenter: wit more than angst

- Melodic Mag: lyrics turn petty grievances into poetry; conversational, as if confiding; vivid concrete details. Highlights "Nobody's Son" and a bridge on "Go Go Juice" that feels like a half-coherent bar conversation. [R] — *Sabrina Carpenter makes petty sound like poetry on Man's Best Friend*. https://www.melodicmag.com/news/sabrina-carpenter-makes-petty-sound-like-poetry-on-mans-best-friend-album-review
- Grammy.com: "whipsmart", slapstick wit that undercuts emotional moments, 12 tracks circling one breakup concept; humour instead of sustained sadness. [R] — *Man's Best Friend review takeaways*. https://cms.grammy.com/sabrina-carpenter-mans-best-friend-review-new-album-takeaways/
- *Short n' Sweet* reception per Wikipedia: Pitchfork's Quinn Moreland admired "diamond-sharp" humour; Rolling Stone and Slant named humour the most striking trait; Slate's Carl Wilson called her a poet laureate of sex; Line of Best Fit said the writing is on trend with relatable subjects and "memeable punchlines"; Telegraph: glib comedian with emotional counterweights. [2] — *Short n' Sweet*, Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_n%27_Sweet
- *Man's Best Friend* reception per Wikipedia: Slant (Sal Cinquemani) found depth lacking and the wordplay dulling; Guardian (Shaad D'Souza) noted over-reliance on profanity and repeated ideas but mostly finely crafted; Independent said songs repeat themes; Pitchfork called it her peak but edging to self-parody. Amy Allen co-wrote all 12. [2] — *Man's Best Friend*, Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man%27s_Best_Friend_(Sabrina_Carpenter_album)

### 1E. Taylor Swift: the template, and the verbosity charge

- NYT (Lindsay Zoladz) on *TTPD*: "unrestrained, imprecise and unnecessarily verbose"; "Great poets know how to condense, or at least how to edit." Pitchfork (Olivia Horn, 6.6) said the songwriting was "conspicuously wanting for an editor". NME 3/5 "cringeworthy" in places. [2] — *Taylor Swift's TTPD: 5 of the Most Brutal Reviews*, Newsweek. https://www.newsweek.com/taylor-swift-bad-reviews-tortured-poets-1892938
- Wikipedia TTPD reception: Exclaim said many tracks "confuse verbosity with poetry"; Guardian (Petridis) called it her wittiest lyrical work; Rolling Stone (Sheffield) loved the chaotic unfiltered quality; Antonoff production called uninventive by Zoladz, Stereogum, NME; Dessner's folk/chamber side favoured by New Yorker (Amanda Petrusich). [2] — *The Tortured Poets Department*, Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tortured_Poets_Department
- Is she a poet? Ann Powers called her "pop's leading writer of autofiction"; poets quoted say the album works more as songwriting than poetry because of choruses and rhyme. [R] — *Taylor Swift: The Tortured Poets Department album poetry review*, Dazed. https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/62460/1/taylor-swift-the-tortured-poets-department-album-poetry-review
- The Ringer on scholars: Swift's songs are "deeply personal, but not private"; linked to Plath/Rich confessional poets, Dickinson, and also to internet "Instapoetry", which some see as a gateway to classic work. This is the only direct Instapoetry link I found in a mainstream outlet, and it's about Swift, not the younger cohort. [R] — *Taylor Swift, poetry and the academy*, The Ringer, Feb 2024. https://www.theringer.com/music/2024/2/13/24071686/taylor-swift-tortured-poets-department-lyrics-poetry-studies

Essay discusses a 2017 Stephanie Burt remark, so partly pre-range. [R] — *Taylor Swift is a Barbarian*, Plume. https://plumepoetry.com/?p=9760

Slant (reviewer says her persona is openly artifice and the songwriting can't lift ordinary-life subjects). Spectator: lyrics dull and repetitive. [2 / R] — Newsweek roundup https://newsweek.com/entertainment/music/taylor-swift-life-of-a-showgirl-reviews-what-to-know-10822349 ; Slant https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/taylor-swift-the-life-of-a-showgirl-album-review/ ; Spectator https://spectator.com/article/has-taylor-swift-lost-it/
- "The Fate of Ophelia" critical split: Clash and Independent called it literary and confessional; Zoladz (NYT) found the Ophelia framing dispiriting. [2] — *The Fate of Ophelia*, Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fate_of_Ophelia

### 1G. Talk-singing, "sad girl" as a label

- Songwriter Colin Munroe counted talk-singing on the Hot 100 roughly doubling in 2024 (about 2.5% to 5% of songs), citing Eilish/Charli xcx "Guess", Chappell Roan's "Hot To Go!", Rodrigo's "Bad Idea Right?", Sabrina's "Please Please Please". His scoring used ChatGPT, so treat as indicative only. [R] — *Talk-singing in 2024*, substack. https://colinmunroe.substack.com/p/talk-singing-in-2024
- Hit Songs Deconstructed (pre-range, 2021 *Sour* data): Rodrigo's verse-2 delivery on one hit shares a conversational quasi-spoken feel with a Swift song; the bridge of "deja vu" is nearly identical to Swift's "Cruel Summer". [R] — https://reports.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/articles/olivia-rodrigo-hits-notable-commonalities/
- Grammy.com on "sad girl pop": roots in Alanis/Fiona Apple, Lana, Swift; intimate diary-like songs; therapy-speak link via artists discussing mental health openly; critique that the label can trivialise women's real pain. Names Abrams, McRae, Rodrigo, Eilish, plus male Conan Gray and Lewis Capaldi. Date unknown. [R] — https://cms.grammy.com/sad-girl-pop-olivia-rodrigo-billie-eilish-gracie-abrams-mental-health-music-psychology-analysis/

### 1H. Explicit comparisons: Instagram poetry / Rupi Kaur, Joni Mitchell, Swift as template

**Instagram poetry / Rupi Kaur**
- Defining the genre (context, pre-range): London Review of Books (Clare Bucknell, 2020) says instapoems are minimalist, brief lines, mostly no capitals or punctuation, rare rhyme, sparse imagery; messages so general they resemble horoscope advice. [R] — *Instapoetry*, LRB. https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v42/n10/clare-bucknell/instapoetry
- Wikipedia's Instapoetry entry lists short direct lines, no consistent meter, self-focus and self-care themes, built for feeds; critics say it values popularity over craft. It mentions no singers. [R] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instapoetry
- Result of searching for pop-lyric / Instapoetry comparisons for this cohort 2023–26: nothing from the named outlets. Closest: The Ringer and Plume (Swift only, above) and Pajiba's charge that Abrams offers a "simulacrum of realness" (a smooth, relatable, generic vulnerability, which echoes the Instapoetry complaint but never names it). [R / 2]
- Observation (mine): critics' sharpest complaint about Swift is the opposite of Instapoetry: too many words (Zoladz, Horn, Exclaim). Rodrigo and Hobert are praised for long, specific, funny lines. So "short, plain, aphoristic" does not describe what these writers or their critics describe.

**Joni Mitchell and the confessional lineage**
- Telegraph (McCormick) compared Eilish's *HMHAS* to *Blue*; Clash heard Elliott Smith, Joni Mitchell and Patti Smith in "Risk"; Rolling Stone CA noted a sly nod to Joni in a *Daughter From Hell* line. [2 / R] — Wikipedia HMHAS, Wikipedia "Risk (song)" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_(song) ; RS CA link above.
- Atwood essay traces a feminine confessional line from Alanis Morissette and Fiona Apple through Regina Spektor, Adrianne Lenker, Lana Del Rey to Swift, Rodrigo, Abrams and Bridgers; asks whether Swift's influence is overstated; no Joni, no Instapoetry. [R] — Atwood essay link above.
- Swift as template from the artists themselves: Gracie credits Swift's advice to write from honesty and says Swift's friendship/homesickness writing broadened what younger writers felt allowed to write about. [R] — Alt Press link above.

## 2. DOES THIS STYLE DOMINATE? AND WHAT ELSE IS BIG

### 2A. Evidence it is big (the support)

- Hit Songs Deconstructed 2025 year-end (Hot 100 top 10, 64 songs): introspection reached 42% of songs, "its highest level in over a decade"; pop's share of top-10 hits rebounded to 42% (from 29%), driven largely by Swift; a post-chorus "hook center" in 63% of hits. [R] — *State of the Hot 100 Top 10, 2025 Year End Report Highlights*. https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/The-State-of-the-Hot-100-Top-10-2025-Year-End-Report-Highlights.pdf ; teaser https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/hsd_wire/the-state-of-the-hot-100-top-10-2025-year-in-review/
- Spring 2026 chart commentary: women held 7 of the Hot 100 top 10 for five straight weeks (longest streak of this kind since 2014), but the leaders were Ella Langley (country) and Olivia Dean (soul-pop), plus HUNTR/X, PinkPantheress/Zara Larsson and Kehlani. [R] — Top 40 Weekly, 25 Apr 2026. https://top40weekly.com/chart-commentary-2026-04-25/
- Atwood essay calls diaristic confessional writing a dominant mode in indie and pop, while arguing authenticity is performance. [R] — link in 1A.

### 2B. Counter-evidence: the numbers

- Billboard Year-End Hot 100 2025 (Nov 2024 to Nov 2025): #1 Lady Gaga/Bruno Mars "Die With a Smile"; #2 Kendrick Lamar/SZA "Luther"; #3 Shaboozey; #4 Teddy Swims; #5 Billie Eilish; #6 Benson Boone; #7 Alex Warren; #8 Post Malone ft. Morgan Wallen; #9 Rosé/Bruno Mars; #10 Chappell Roan. [R] — Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_2025
- **My own tally from that list** (not a critic's claim): across all 100, the named cohort leads about 17 entries: Sabrina 5, Billie 2, Chappell 2, sombr 2, Tate McRae 2, Gracie 1 ("That's So True" #14), Lola Young 1, Gigi Perez 1, Alex Warren 1. Add two features (Tate McRae on a Wallen song, Lola Young on a Tyler, the Creator song) for 19. Olivia Rodrigo and Taylor Swift have none; only 3 of the top 10 are cohort. The rest is rap/R&B, country (Morgan Wallen appears on about 10 entries, as lead or feature), Latin (Bad Bunny 4), K-pop (the *KPop Demon Hunters* songs about 7, plus Rosé and Jimin), Gaga, Teddy Swims, Benson Boone, and four perennial Christmas songs.
- Luminate 2025 (AP): US on-demand streams by genre: rap/R&B about 350 billion (more than one in four); rock about 260 billion (about 19%); pop about 167 billion (about 12%); country about 122 billion (about 9%); Latin about 121 billion (about 9%). Fastest growth: Christian/gospel +18.5%, rock +6.4%, Latin +5.2%. Share percentages are the fetch tool's own arithmetic from rounded totals. [R] — *Music streams hit 5 trillion in 2025*, AP via WTOP. https://wtop.com/national/2026/01/music-streams-hit-5-trillion-in-2025-christian-rock-and-latin-lead-growth-in-the-us/
- Luminate mid-2026 (reported): top 10 songs include Ella Langley, Olivia Dean, HUNTR/X, PinkPantheress/Zara Larsson and Swift; men in the top 10 songs are only Alex Warren, Bad Bunny, Bruno Mars; but men do better on albums (Morgan Wallen #1, Noah Kahan, BTS, Bad Bunny). None of the main "diary pop" names (Abrams, Rodrigo, Carpenter) is in the reported top 10 songs. [R] — *Halfway through 2026, female artists are tops*. https://www.lakesmedianetwork.com/ac/halfway-through-2026-female-artists-are-tops-says-new-report/

### 2C. Counter-evidence: critics on the other big things

- **Male earnest balladeers.** Rolling Stone (AU) argues hits by Alex Warren, Benson Boone and Teddy Swims follow a talent-show formula: sparse backing, a big octave leap inside the first minute, built for TikTok and The Voice. Gives chart durations (e.g. "Lose Control" 88 weeks). It's not framed as gendered by the writer. [R] — *Why does everything sound like a Voice audition now?* https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/voice-audition-songs-alex-warren-benson-boone-76636
- AP year-end 2024: the boys also broke out: Teddy Swims reached #1, Benson Boone #2, Hozier returned. [R] — *The Year in Music 2024: The boys break out*. https://www.easy931miami.com/news/year-music-2024-boys/TEZ5UU2YUZBSV4V5IEDTAIWWRU/
- **Country.** UK (BPI): country's share of singles hit 3.3% in 2024 versus under 1% in every year before 2019, driven by Shaboozey, Dasha, Beyoncé, Post Malone/Wallen. [R] — Music Week. https://www.musicweek.com/public_html/index.php/labels/read/bpi-country-music-doubles-share-of-singles-market-in-two-years/091863 . US: Ella Langley's "Choosin' Texas" hit #1 in Feb 2026, first woman to top Hot 100, Hot Country Songs and Country Airplay; Stereogum calls a country #1 "historically uncommon" but more frequent in the 2020s boom. [R] — https://stereogum.com/2488632/ella-langley-scores-first-1-hit-with-choosin-texas/news/
- **K-pop / soundtracks.** "Golden" from *KPop Demon Hunters* spent 8 weeks at #1, first #1 by a female K-pop act, four soundtrack songs in the top 10 at once, soundtrack over 15 billion streams; won the Oscar. [R] — *A "Golden" year: KPop Demon Hunters by the numbers*. https://www.lakesmedianetwork.com/ac/a-golden-year-kpop-demon-hunters-by-the-numbers/
- **Maximalist / dance pop.** The Spinoff (June 2024): the pandemic-era hush (Swift's introspection, Eilish for headphones, Rodrigo's 90s indie) is being replaced by Chappell Roan, Sabrina Carpenter and Charli xcx as big, melodic, sexually frank, euphoric pop; Lorde's raw reply to Charli suggests confession survives inside danceable songs. ### Gracie Abrams
- "That's So True" started as a bonus track on the deluxe *Secret of Us* (Oct 2024), went viral on TikTok via an acoustic clip with Hobert, then became her first Hot 100 top 10 and first UK #1. ## 4. NAMED TECHNIQUES: GRACIE ABRAMS / DESSNER / HOBERT AND OLIVIA RODRIGO / NIGRO

### 4A. Gracie Abrams, Aaron Dessner, Audrey Hobert

- **Journaling as raw material.** Gracie says she carries journals everywhere and calls journaling her best songwriting advice because it preserves details you'd forget; she describes writing as sometimes "pulled out of the air". [R] — *The Writer's Block: Gracie Abrams*, American Songwriter. https://americansongwriter.com/the-writers-block-gracie-abrams-on-finding-herself-through-songwriting/?nb=1
- **Speed and volume.** For *Good Riddance* she worked 12-hour days at Dessner's Long Pond studio and wrote up to two songs a day; "Amelie" began as a journal entry and was captured in one take with Dessner on guitar. [2] — Wikipedia Good Riddance (1A).
- **Letting the funniest line win.** On "I Love You, I'm Sorry" (Song Exploder #283), lyrics came quickly and the pair kept whichever line made them laugh; many early lines were dropped. [R] — Song Exploder transcript. https://songexploder.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Song-Exploder-Gracie-Abrams-Transcript.pdf
- **Bridge built for contrast and mouthfeel.** On that song the bridge was drafted as mumbled, speech-like phrasing, written for how words feel in the mouth rather than meaning, and designed to break from the verses rhythmically; a spontaneous ad-lib survived from the demo. [R] — same Song Exploder transcript.
- **Title as a family of titles.** The title copies the shape of her earlier "I Miss You, I'm Sorry", written about the same ex, after years of trying variants. [R] — same transcript.
- **Dessner's role.** Plays or produces fast, often single-take: one-take acoustic guitar, then lap steel, bass, synths and strings added; co-writes some songs ("Humming" with Justin Vernon); a near-improvised vocal on "Cold Goodbyes". He says they rarely discuss who songs are about and he finds leaving it unsaid more interesting. [R] — Song Exploder transcript; Billboard https://ca.billboard.com/music/music-news/aaron-dessner-gracie-abrams-death-wish-noah-kahan-interview-1236299956/
- **Hobert's method.** Outlines lyrics like monologues from a dramatic-writing background, edits for clarity and rhythm; the hook for her own "Sue Me" came while venting to her producer and singing the line immediately; wants songs to be both candid and catchy. [R] — W Magazine. https://www.wmagazine.com/culture/audrey-hobert-sue-me-whos-the-clown-album-interview
- **"That's So True" provenance.** First draft written on a rooftop with Hobert, drunk, laughing and crying, original lyrics much rawer; finished with Dessner at Electric Lady; Gracie says Hobert is a great wordplay and sound-of-words writer. [R] — Nylon link in section 3.
- **Specificity then extremity.** Atwood panel: starts from real stories and pushes them to extremes; outros escalate line by line. Gracie says she loves songs specific enough that there's little room for interpretation, at some cost to universality. [R] — Atwood roundtable (1A); *Gracie Abrams and Olivia Rodrigo in conversation*, Interview. https://interviewmagazine.com/music/gracie-abrams-and-olivia-rodrigo-plan-their-month-long-sleepover
- **Accountability move.** She now says she writes herself as both protagonist and antagonist and regrets earlier carelessness about the people songs describe; Rodrigo says her own instinct is to pour everything out. [R] — Billboard, *Olivia Rodrigo and Gracie Abrams address careless songwriting*. https://ca.billboard.com/music/music-news/olivia-rodrigo-gracie-abrams-address-careless-songwriting-1236289033/
- **Critics' view of the technique.** Where critics pan her, it's for vagueness or sameness rather than lack of intimacy (Pitchfork/Guardian on *Daughter From Hell* via Pajiba; Atwood essay on lack of specificity; Line of Best Fit "hyperspecific and vague").
- **Names and specifics:** I found no critic discussing her use of named people or places specifically. The tangible-detail point comes from Alt Press (domestic details) and the Atwood pieces. Not found otherwise.

### 4B. Olivia Rodrigo and Dan Nigro

- **Lyric-first split.** Rodrigo owns the words; Nigro the melody, chords and texture; he trims lines that feel too wordy or too sparse and balances phrasing. [R, pre-range 2021] — Variety, *Olivia Rodrigo, Dan Nigro and the Sour process*. https://variety.com/2021/music/news/olivia-rodrigo-dan-nigro-sour-songwriting-production-process-1235033768
- **Seeds from concept or literature.** Rodrigo says she usually starts alone, often with a concept or poem, and lyrics and melody tend to arrive together; "All-American Bitch" was inspired by a Joan Didion essay title. [R / 2] — Interview (above); Wikipedia All-American Bitch.
- **Rewrite until each line has a job.** For "vampire" they spent a full day testing small lyric changes; the central hook was already in her seed; the bridge was written about a week after the core track; early listeners said it sounded like three songs in one, which led him to smooth the section joins. [R] — Sound on Sound, *Inside Track: Olivia Rodrigo, "Vampire"*. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-olivia-rodrigo-vampire ; Grammy.com https://grammy.com/news/dan-nigro-interview-olivia-rodrigo-producer-of-the-year-2024-grammys
- **Move sections around.** On "Get Him Back!" an early verse became the bridge. On "ballad of a homeschooled girl" verse and chorus came in about ten minutes from chords and a vocal, saved as a voice memo, finished months later. [R] — Grammy.com (above).
- **Demo vocal sometimes stays.** If the demo vocal is good enough they keep it; otherwise they fix lyrics and melody first, then record. Demo on upright piano and iPhone. [R] — Sound on Sound.
- **Argue arrangement around the lyric.** On "drivers license" Rodrigo wanted a sparser bridge and Nigro fuller verses; they tested both for days. [R, pre-range] — Variety.
- **Verse-to-chorus transition.** Nigro says the moment a verse hands over to the chorus is crucial and may take days. [R] — Universal Audio. https://www.uaudio.com/blogs/ua/apollo-creators-daniel-nigro
- **Chappell Roan with Nigro.** Back-and-forth; he co-wrote the bridge and reworked the verse lyrics on "Red Wine Supernova"; "Femininomenon" got a theatrical shift and a deliberate fake-out opening to hold a four-minute track; "Hot to Go!" took about two hours; write first, produce after. [R] — NPR via WAMC https://www.wamc.org/2024-12-23/how-chappell-roans-producer-dan-nigro-crafts-pop-hits-for-a-new-generation ; Rolling Stone https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/dan-nigro-chappell-roan-olivia-rodrigo-grammys-interview-podcast-1235204396/
- **Title as album concept.** She had GUTS in mind as a title since *Sour*, from the phrase "spill your guts". [2] — Wikipedia GUTS (1C). For single-song titles as hooks, I found no interview where Nigro discusses it. Not found.
- **Structural habits critics counted (pre-range, Sour):** hook at the end of the chorus; eight of 11 songs are breakups; the ex and his new partner introduced in verse 1; car/driving imagery; bridges borrowed in shape from Swift. [R] — Hit Songs Deconstructed (1G).
- **Critics on the technique.** NYT, Guardian, Rolling Stone and others praise the wit and blunt self-analysis; Clash calls the lyrics complex and verbose; Pitchfork felt the ballads repeated her earlier work; AP praised the concept-album shape of OR3.

### 4C. Related songwriter-side notes (helpful for contrast)
- Amy Allen (Sabrina Carpenter's main co-writer): says the lines that feel "too deep and too specific or niche" often resonate most; she discovered humour in her writing through Sabrina; follows the "dare to suck" idea; her own album was written linearly from first word to last. [R] — NPR via KUNR https://www.kunr.org/2025-12-26/pop-hitmaker-amy-allen-tries-to-lean-in-to-feeling-uncomfortable-in-her-songwriting ; Marie Claire https://www.marieclaire.com/culture/music/amy-allen-interview/ ; Nylon https://www.nylon.com/entertainment/amy-allen-debut-album-songwriting-sabrina-carpenter
- Sabrina Carpenter: says she waited to write about mature subjects until it felt authentic and wrote with a small circle of trusted collaborators. [R] — Rolling Stone AU. https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/sabrina-carpenter-espresso-short-n-sweet-taylor-swift-interview-61709

## 5. WHAT THIS SUPPORTS / CONTRADICTS IN HIS HYPOTHESIS (10 lines)

1. SUPPORTS: critics do constantly use diary, confessional, "late-night text" and "Notes app" language for Abrams, Rodrigo, Eilish, Swift and Lorde, and name Swift as the template (Atwood, PopMatters, Alt Press, Rolling Stone).
2. SUPPORTS: the 2025 Hot 100 top 10 shows introspective lyrics at 42% of songs, a decade high (Hit Songs Deconstructed), and Rodrigo and Abrams are both repeatedly called conversational and talk-sung.
3. SUPPORTS (craft): the bridge-as-climax idea is explicitly backed: Gracie calls bridges underrated, critics single out her bridges, Nigro writes bridges late, and "That's So True" went viral via its bridge.
4. SUPPORTS (sameness): the 2026 pushback on *Daughter From Hell* (Guardian, Pitchfork via Pajiba; Dazed; Hot Press) is that the register has become vague and interchangeable, which is close to his worry.
5. CONTRADICTS "Instagram poet": I found no critic making that comparison for this cohort, and the mainstream critique of the best-known writers is verbosity and too many words (Swift), or length and comic detail (Rodrigo, Hobert), the opposite of Instapoetry's short aphorisms.
6. CONTRADICTS "all relationship angst": the biggest female-led material is humour (Sabrina), camp and euphoria (Chappell, Charli), love songs (Olivia Dean, Rodrigo's 2026 album), or girlhood chaos (UnHerd), not one angst mode.
7. CONTRADICTS "most songs": by my count about 17 of the 2025 year-end Hot 100's 100 songs are led by his named cohort, 3 of the top 10; rap/R&B is over a quarter of US streams, pop only about 12% (Luminate).
8. CONTRADICTS: what else is big is large and varied: male earnest ballads (Warren, Boone, Swims), country (Wallen, Shaboozey, Ella Langley #1 in 2026), K-pop (HUNTR/X 8 weeks at #1), Latin (Bad Bunny), Gaga/Bruno and Kendrick/SZA.
9. NUANCE: women led 7 of the top 10 for five weeks in spring 2026, but via country and soul-pop; and diaristic male writers (sombr, Noah Kahan, Alex Warren) are also big, so "female" is not the defining variable.
10. NUANCE (album tracks): sweeps and deep-cut hits (Swift's top-14 and top-12 weeks, "Birds of a Feather", "Chihiro", GUTS tracks) reflect superfan release-week streaming and shout-along design as much as the style itself; the best-attested viral album cut (a bridge, deluxe bonus track, TikTok) is from Gracie.
